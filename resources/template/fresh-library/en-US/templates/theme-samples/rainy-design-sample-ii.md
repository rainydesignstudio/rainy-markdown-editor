The evening rain fell softly. At the corner, a shop sign appeared twice: once on the wall and once in the puddle, wavering with every step. Only then did the day leave enough space to think.

<section class="my-8">
  <p class="mt-0 mb-2 text-xs font-semibold tracking-widest text-article-muted">01 / OBSERVATION</p>
  <h2 class="mt-0 mb-4 text-2xl font-semibold text-article-heading">A pause at the corner</h2>
  <p class="m-0 text-base text-article-ink">Someone folded an umbrella outside the convenience store, shook off the drops, then pushed the door open. I stayed a moment and listened to tires passing over the wet road.</p>
</section>

I used to think stopping meant falling behind. Some details only appear when you stop rushing: rain on glass, clean leaves and a window with its light still on.

<blockquote class="my-7 border-b-2 border-article-line bg-article-tint px-6 py-5">
  <p class="m-0 text-lg font-medium text-article-heading">The city had not slowed down. I had finally left enough time to notice its usual pace.</p>
</blockquote>

<section class="my-8">
  <p class="mt-0 mb-2 text-xs font-semibold tracking-widest text-article-muted">02 / RETURN</p>
  <h2 class="mt-0 mb-4 text-2xl font-semibold text-article-heading">Leave a light for yourself</h2>
  <p class="m-0 text-base text-article-ink">At home, I set the umbrella by the door and cleared a small space for paper on the desk. The rain outside had stopped. Inside, there was still a quiet sound that suited writing.</p>
</section>

<section class="mt-10 border-b border-article-line pb-5">
  <p class="mt-0 mb-3 text-sm text-article-ink">May you find a little time tonight that does not need to hurry.</p>
